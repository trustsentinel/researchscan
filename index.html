Meridian — blockchain network measurement

You found this page because Meridian connected to your node. It is a non-commercial PhD research project measuring how centralised public blockchain peer-to-peer networks really are.

What it is

A doctoral study in network security and internet measurement. It maps the infrastructure that public blockchain networks actually run on — hosting providers, network operators, software versions, geography — to measure how concentrated, and therefore how fragile, those networks are.

Ten networks, four vantage points, every day. Findings are published as aggregates and statistics. Your node is one data point among hundreds of thousands; it is never the subject of the research.

What it does — and never does

✓ It does

  • Make a standard peer-to-peer connection and handshake, and read what your node already advertises — software version, capabilities, block height
  • Ask for peer addresses, the way the protocol itself provides for
  • Check whether a short, fixed list of well-known ports is open, and read what an already-open service announces to anyone who connects
  • Contact a node at most about once a week per check, with five-second timeouts

✗ It never

  • Logs in, authenticates, or accesses anything non-public
  • Sends transactions or blocks, or anything meant to change your node's state
  • Exploits, fuzzes, or tests vulnerabilities against your node
  • Touches a port that was not already open, or runs those checks against a .onion hidden service
